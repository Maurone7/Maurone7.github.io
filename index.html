A 6-DOF Gravity Offloading System

A 9 m × 4 m gantry that moves a payload through three translational and three rotational degrees of freedom, so satellites, docking mechanisms and in-space assembly hardware can be tested under orbit-like kinematics on Earth.

Structure

Frame

80×80 mm aluminium T-slot legs bolted to the floor carry two 9 m sides of 160×80 mm profile, topped with extra-wide ball-bearing rails that guide the gantry and stiffen the frame. Gussets and diagonal braces support the 6 m spans.

Analysis

Bending FEA

Fusion simulations of the 6 m beams: 4.27 mm maximum deflection at 2000 N and 7.24 mm at 4000 N, with von Mises stress of 111 and 192 MPa.

X-axis · 8 m travel

Dual rack-and-pinion gantry

Both ends of the cross beam are driven by their own Teknic NEMA 34 servo, 50:1 gearbox and DIN6 helical rack, so the beam doesn't skew and bind when it accelerates. Motor torque is capped at 54% to protect the gearbox if the axis jams.

Sizing

Performance envelope

Each axis is sized against motor peak torque, reflected inertia, gearbox E-stop ratings, rack force and bearing moments. Available acceleration drops steeply as payload mass grows.

Scaling orbits down to the lab

No single configuration on the ground preserves orbital period, angular velocity, gravity gradient, Coriolis effects and structural loads at once. Matching GEO at the system's 1.75 m radius, for example, would need an orbital period of about 0.7 µs.

The system is therefore treated as a hybrid simulator, and the scaling law is chosen to fit the experiment:

  • Angular-velocity scaling — GEO, MEO and LEO angular rates need only 3.8–61 RPM at the motor, all well within limits.
  • Reduced-radius acceleration — a 0.18 m synthetic orbit reproduces GEO centripetal acceleration.
  • Time-scaled trajectories — the sequence and relative timing of control events is preserved.
  • Active emulation — closed-loop centripetal, Coriolis and gravity-gradient acceleration fields.

First experiment: a circular orbit in X–Y

Where it started · M.S. research

GRIP: a Modular Gravity Off-Loading Testbed

The Gravity-offloaded RobotIcs and Proximity operations testbed is a 5-DOF prototype for testing proximity robotics, such as servicing, assembly and docking, and for reduced-gravity rover terrain tests. Its track-based gantry lets several payloads ride the same rails at once and interact with each other with sub-millimeter precision.

  • Cartesian gantry with 2 translational DOF, built from 8020 aluminium and a gear-based X–Y drive.
  • In-house 3-axis gimbal that holds objects of arbitrary shape, cylinders especially, and rotates them about an arbitrary axis.
  • Modular by design: each subsystem is itself an off-loader. The Y-axis was later extended to 5 ft by swapping in longer beams, with no other parts reworked.
  • Raspberry Pi 5 control of Teknic ClearPath servos with encoder feedback, logging and multi-threaded command streaming.
  • Mostly 3D-printed motor holders, racks, gears and gimbal rings, printed on a Prusa XL and checked with FEM.
