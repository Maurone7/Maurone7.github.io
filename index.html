Current research · Ph.D.

GRIP: A 6-DOF Gravity Offloading Facility

A 9 m × 4 m × 3 m gantry facility that positions a payload in three translational and three rotational degrees of freedom. The system enables ground-based testing of satellites, docking mechanisms, and in-space assembly hardware under representative orbital kinematics.

Subsystems

Structure

Frame

Four floor-anchored 80×80 mm aluminum T-slot columns support two 9 m longitudinal members of 160×80 mm profile. Extra-wide ball-bearing rails mounted on these members guide the gantry and increase structural stiffness. Gussets and diagonal braces reinforce the 6 m spans.

Analysis

Structural Analysis

Finite-element analysis of the 6 m beams in Autodesk Fusion predicts maximum deflections of 4.27 mm and 7.24 mm, and peak von Mises stresses of 111 MPa and 192 MPa, under loads of 2000 N and 4000 N, respectively.

X-axis · 8 m travel

Dual Rack-and-Pinion Drive

Each end of the cross beam is driven by an independent Teknic NEMA 34 servo motor, 50:1 gearbox, and DIN 6 helical rack. Synchronized actuation eliminates the moment that would otherwise cause racking and carriage binding during acceleration. Motor torque is limited to 54% of peak to remain within the gearbox emergency-stop rating.

Scaling of Orbital Conditions

GRIP operates as a hybrid simulator: the scaling criterion is selected for each experiment, allowing LEO, MEO, and GEO conditions to be emulated within the laboratory workspace.

  • Angular-velocity scaling: GEO, MEO, and LEO angular rates require motor speeds of only 3.8–61 RPM, well within actuator limits.
  • Reduced-radius acceleration scaling: a 0.18 m synthetic orbit reproduces GEO centripetal acceleration.
  • Time scaling: preserves the sequence and relative timing of maneuvers and control events.
  • Active emulation: closed-loop generation of centripetal, Coriolis, and gravity-gradient acceleration fields.

Experimental Validation: Circular Trajectory

A circular trajectory in the X–Y plane was approximated by a 52-sided polygon and executed through velocity commands issued by a Raspberry Pi 5. Increasing the number of sides reduces the geometric approximation error but increases communication overhead, which can prevent the motors from reaching the commanded velocities.

M.S. research

GRIP Prototype: A Modular Gravity Off-Loading Testbed

The first-generation Gravity-offloaded RobotIcs and Proximity operations (GRIP) testbed is a five-degree-of-freedom prototype for testing proximity robotics, including servicing, assembly, and docking, as well as reduced-gravity terrain testing of planetary rovers. Its track-based translational system allows multiple payloads to be installed simultaneously and to interact with sub-millimeter precision.

Full M.S. thesis: A Modular Gravity Off-Loading System (PDF, Penn State) ↗

  • Cartesian gantry: two translational degrees of freedom, constructed from 8020 aluminum profiles with a gear-driven X–Y mechanism.
  • Three-axis gimbal: designed in-house to accommodate payloads of arbitrary shape, particularly cylindrical bodies, and to rotate them about an arbitrary axis.
  • Modular architecture: each subsystem functions as an independent off-loader. The Y-axis was subsequently extended to 5 ft by replacing only the structural beams.
  • Control system: a Raspberry Pi 5 running multi-threaded C++ commands Teknic ClearPath servo motors with encoder feedback, real-time data acquisition, and multi-threaded command execution.
  • Additive manufacturing: motor mounts, gear racks, gears, and gimbal rings were fabricated on a Prusa XL and verified through finite-element analysis.
